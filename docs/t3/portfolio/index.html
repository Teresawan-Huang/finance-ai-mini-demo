Team interpretation

The frozen candidate (GLD 60% / SPY 40% / TLT 0%) beat the equal-weight baseline on total return in both windows, but the drawdown story is split between the two windows.

Design window (2021-09-01 to 2024-08-30). The selected candidate returned +33.94% versus +12.43% for the baseline, and its maximum drawdown of -17.81% was shallower than the baseline's -22.81%. The selection rule required the design return to be at least as high as the baseline (within 1e-12) and then chose the eligible allocation with the smallest drawdown loss. On those two criteria the candidate was strictly better than the baseline in the design window.

Test window (2024-09-03 to 2026-08-31). The frozen candidate returned +63.30% versus +37.13% for the baseline, so the return advantage persisted and actually widened. Its maximum drawdown (-17.01%) was, however, deeper than the baseline's (-11.64%). The design-period drawdown advantage did not persist: the allocation that minimized drawdown loss in the design window had a worse drawdown in the test window, even though it kept (and extended) the return advantage.

Why the pattern happened. The candidate starts 60% in gold and 0% in bonds. GLD rose strongly across the test window, which drove the higher return; the deeper drawdown reflects periods when the portfolio fell from its running peak. The equal-weight baseline's one-third TLT sleeve provided drawdown damping even though bonds lagged on return.

A condition that could change the conclusion. The candidate's edge depends on GLD continuing to outperform on this return-drawdown basis. If gold entered a sustained drawdown while bonds rallied (for example, a risk-on shift or rising real yields), the heavy gold weight and zero bond weight could make the candidate's drawdown deeper than the baseline's in later windows — a reversal already visible on the drawdown axis of this test. The result also depends on the fixed rules: a different objective, a different grid, a different split of the history, or modeling fund fees and trading costs could select a different candidate.

Glossary

Weight — the fraction of the starting portfolio value invested in one asset, shown as a percentage. A 60% weight in GLD means the portfolio begins with $60 of every $100 in GLD. In this experiment weights are initial allocations: they drift as prices move because the portfolio is held without rebalancing.

Benchmark — the default or reference portfolio used for comparison. Here the benchmark is the equal-weight baseline, which splits the starting value exactly one-third into SPY, TLT and GLD. The research question is whether a different initial allocation can improve the return-drawdown trade-off relative to this benchmark.

Drawdown — how far the portfolio value has fallen from its highest previous value within a window. On each date it is (value / running peak − 1), shown as a percentage. A maximum drawdown of -17.01% means the portfolio at its worst point stood 17.01% below its prior peak. Drawdown is always nonpositive because each new peak resets the running peak; a value nearer zero means a smaller loss from a preceding peak.

Method, source and limits

Search 66 long-only 10%-grid allocations plus the exact equal-weight baseline. Require design return at least equal to the baseline within 1e-12, then minimize drawdown loss. Ties prefer the baseline, higher return, and ascending SPY/TLT/GLD weights. Retain the baseline if there is no strictly smaller loss.

For each window, portfolio value is 100 × the sum of each initial weight × its adjusted-close ratio to the first close in that window. Drawdown is value / running peak − 1. No previous-window return enters the calculation.

Source: the frozen T2 data/t2/daily_prices.csv. See data/t2/data_dictionary.md for provenance and validation limits. Adjusted-close values follow the provider's split/distribution convention. No additional fund-fee deduction, trading cost, slippage or tax is modeled.

Both periods were already available in the course data. This is a retrospective holdout with a procedural freeze, not a blinded market test. Three assets, one historical split and a small grid cannot establish a universally best strategy. Hypothetical results do not guarantee future profits.
